AMENDMENT 3 · ON THE BALLOT NOVEMBER 2026

Amendment 3 would cut Jacksonville's property tax revenue by $300 million a year.

Public safety already takes up more than half of the city's General Fund. Closing a gap this size means dramatic cuts — to public safety, to the services people rely on, or both — or shifting the cost to renters and small businesses instead.

Three things every Jacksonville voter should know

01

Jacksonville is a low-tax city

Jacksonville already invests about half as much as peer cities in its residents. Ours is not a budget with obvious fat to trim.

02

What this costs Jacksonville

Almost $300 million a year in lost General Fund revenue — roughly 15% of the city's entire budget.

03

The choice that creates

Close the gap by making dramatic cuts to public safety or other services citizens rely on, or by shifting the cost onto renters and small businesses. There isn't a third, free option.

What Amendment 3 actually does

Raises the homestead exemption on non-school taxes from $50,000 today to $150,000 in 2027 and $250,000 in 2028.

New residents wait 5 years before qualifying for the higher exemption.

Lowers the non-homestead assessment cap on rental and commercial property from 10% to 5% a year.

Needs 60% voter approval in the November 2026 election.
